# L31 Speaker Notes — Capstone Workshop: IR Simulation

## Delivery Guide
The full four-phase guided exercise (L26's compressed version, now complete):
detection → triage → containment → reporting, on the week-10 incident dataset,
under time pressure, in teams with roles. Your role is timekeeper and Socratic
nudge — intervene only when a team is truly stuck, and let recoverable mistakes
stand (the debrief is where they pay off). The defense-prep brief closes: report
finalization + the defense format.

## Timing Plan
0–15 roles + brief + rules of engagement (corroboration rule in force) · 15–60
phase 1–2 (detect → triage/scope) · 60–70 checkpoint 1 (scope statement reviewed) ·
70–115 phase 3 (containment ladder, documented) · 115–125 break · 125–155 phase 4
(report drafting against the template) · 155–175 checkpoint 2 (draft reviewed) ·
175–190 hot debrief (what held, what broke) · 190–200 defense brief + exit ticket. 
**Compression:** none — this session *is* the capstone's working core; if time
collapses, cut the hot debrief to checkpoint-2 comments only.

## Teaching Demonstrations
No demos — this is facilitation. The three instructor moves:
1. **Checkpoint reviews:** 3 minutes per team, scope statement or draft only; one question each ("what corroborates this?" / "what's your reversal for this block?").
2. **Stuck-team nudge:** point at the method (three-way corroboration, containment ladder), never at the answer.
3. **Time announcements:** at each phase boundary; teams that ignore the clock learn from the debrief, not from rescue.

## Expected Student Difficulties
1. Role collisions (two incident commanders) — roles were assigned in L25; reassign quietly if needed, note it for the debrief.
2. Perfectionism under clock — partial-but-documented beats undocumented-complete; say it at the brief and grade accordingly.
3. Report written at the end in a panic — checkpoint 2 exists to catch this; require the facts section drafted before phase 4 begins.

## Discussion Facilitation
The hot debrief is whole-class: three questions only — *what held?* (method), *what
broke?* (process or evidence gap), *what will you do differently in the defense?*
Keep it blameless (L27's norms); the lessons-learned sentence stems are on the
board.

## Lab Troubleshooting (simulation context)
- Dataset access failures: read-only copies per team prepared in advance; custody forms (L29) apply.
- A team "finishes" early: their containment justification gets challenged — early usually means under-scoped.
- Escalation confusion: the L25 roster and severity rubric are posted; point, don't re-teach.

## Accessibility Notes
- Time pressure + role play: extended-clock option and non-speaking analyst roles documented; written brief and templates for all.
- Team composition: mix communication strengths deliberately; the roster (not volunteers) assigns roles.

## CS & Data Science Applications
- **CS:** the exercise is distributed-systems incident response — coordination, state handoff, and rollback under failure; CS students should feel at home.
- **DS:** scope statements are queries over evidence; confidence labels are calibrated classification — DS students own the corroboration math.
