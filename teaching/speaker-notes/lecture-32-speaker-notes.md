# L32 Speaker Notes — Capstone Defense & Course Synthesis

## Delivery Guide
Defense day. Your job: run a fair, rubric-anchored defense, then close the course
with the synthesis that makes 32 lectures cohere. Defenses are 10 minutes + 5
questions; the rubric (distributed at L30) is the only criterion — no vibes.
The synthesis maps the semester's through-lines (defense-in-depth, trust
boundaries, evidence discipline, cryptography as enabler, measurement) onto the
modules students lived. Course evals and final logistics close.

## Timing Plan
0–10 defense format + rubric reminder + order drawn · 10–100 defenses (7 teams ×
10+5, strict clock) · 100–110 break · 110–130 remaining defenses (if >7 teams,
adjust: 6 teams pre-defense + rubric recalibration note in manual) · 130–150
course synthesis (the five through-lines) · 150–160 final-exam logistics +
capstone report submission deadline reminder · 160–170 course evaluations +
close. **Compression:** defenses never compress; the synthesis can go to 12
minutes by pointing at the syllabus map rather than re-teaching.

## Teaching Demonstrations
1. **Defense facilitation:** one question per panelist, rubric-referenced ("walk me through the corroboration for this claim" — not "why did you choose X?" alone); note-taking grid per team.
2. **Synthesis walkthrough:** the five through-lines each anchored to one lecture moment students will remember (the L06 ARP table, the L13 nonce, the L19 SG deny, the L26 ladder, the L24 inversion).
3. **Rubric calibration:** first defense scored by all panelists independently, then reconciled — consistency before speed.

## Expected Student Difficulties
1. Defense nerves — the format was briefed twice (L30, L31); rehearse-the-first-90-seconds advice given; start with the scope statement everyone has.
2. Overrunning the clock — the timekeeper's visible countdown; overruns eat their own Q&A, not the schedule.
3. Team imbalance surfacing publicly — the rubric includes individual contribution; private follow-up if a defense reveals it.

## Discussion Facilitation
Panel questions probe method, not memorization: "what corroborates this?", "what
breaks if the egress deny lands before the observation window?", "which finding
would you escalate first and why?" — the same verbs the course taught. Avoid
gotchas; the goal is evidence-of-learning, not stump-the-chump.

## Lab Troubleshooting (defense logistics)
- Slides fail: HDMI + PDF fallback pre-checked; teams submit decks the night before (checkpoint enforced).
- Team member absent: defense proceeds with the rubric's individual-contribution adjustment documented; no deferral without instructor approval.
- Rubric drift across panelists: the calibration defense (first slot) exists for this; reconcile before slot two.

## Accessibility Notes
- Presenting under evaluation: alternative format (recorded defense with live Q&A after) documented in the manual's accessibility section; requested in advance, not discovered on the day.
- Seating, lighting, and the countdown visibility checked before the first defense.

## CS & Data Science Applications
- **CS:** defending an architecture is a design review — trade-off articulation and failure-mode honesty are the same muscles as code review.
- **DS:** the report's measurement section (metrics, confidence, baselines) is a model-evaluation writeup — DS students present it as such.
