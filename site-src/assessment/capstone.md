# Capstone Project

The capstone (15%) integrates the entire course: your team acts as a
security consultancy for a **simulated organization** — producing a full
security assessment, a design, an exercised incident-response plan, and a
defended report, across weeks 9–16.

> All capstone organizations are simulated fiction. No real infrastructure
> is scanned, tested, or contacted at any point.

## The deliverable arc

| Milestone | Week | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Brief issued | 9 | Team formation + organization profile (one of four: startup, hospital, university, data-science platform) |
| Assessment | 11–12 | Asset/threat/risk assessment mapped to CLO-1..CLO-10 lenses |
| Design | 13 | Segmentation, secure-access, and detection architecture with tradeoffs |
| Exercise | 14 | IR tabletop or simulation your team designs and runs |
| Draft report | 15 | Full findings + remediation roadmap (peer-review swap in class) |
| Defense | 16 | 25-minute oral defense + final report submission |

## What the defense looks like

- 10 minutes presenting the design and its tradeoffs (not a feature tour).
- 15 minutes of structured challenge: the panel probes containment
  ordering, failure modes, and cost/complexity realism — the same five
  question archetypes rehearsed in the course cases.
- Individual scores: your teammates' report is shared; your defense is not.

## Assessment

The 100-point rubric weights: technical correctness of the assessment,
design defensibility (explicit tradeoffs, honest limitations), exercise
quality (do injects actually test the runbook?), report clarity, and the
defense itself. The full rubric and band anchors are distributed with the
brief; grading keys remain instructor-only.

**Start from the course's own tools:** the
[case studies](../cases/index.md) expert tier (cs-091–100) is literally the
capstone arc rehearsed case-by-case — assessment → roadmap → design →
board report → exercise → defense.
