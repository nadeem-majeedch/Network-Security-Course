# Examinations

Two written examinations anchor the course: the **midterm** (week 8,
modules 1–4) and the **final** (week 16, synthesis + forensic practical).
Both are closed-book, individually answered, and marked against published
marking schemes — not curve-adjusted impressions.

## Midterm — week 8 · 100 marks · 2 hours

| Section | Marks | Format |
|---|---|---|
| A | 20 | MCQ — protocol mechanics, threat classification |
| B | 30 | Short answer — subnet/ACL/trust-zone reasoning |
| C | 30 | Scenario analysis — one incident across modules 2–3 |
| D | 20 | Diagram interpretation — segmentation + firewall placement |

Scope: modules 1–4 (protocol foundations → VPN architecture). The week-8
self-check quiz is the shape preview.

## Final — week 16 · 100 marks · 2.5 hours

| Part | Marks | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Written | 60 | Synthesis across all eight modules; two integrative scenarios |
| Forensic practical | 40 | Course-dataset evidence: handling → timeline reconstruction → attack-path synthesis → defensible report |

The forensic practical uses the same evidence disciplines as
[Lab-15](../labs/lab-15-forensic-timeline-report.md) — if you can defend
that lab's timeline, the practical is a rehearsed skill, not a surprise.

## How exams are marked

- Section-wise model answers and mark allocation live in the instructor
  marking schemes (never published).
- Partial credit follows stated reasoning: a wrong conclusion reached via
  sound, verifiable steps earns more than a lucky guess with no method.
- The capstone defense (week 16) is assessed separately — see
  [Capstone](capstone.md).

## Integrity

Closed book, no devices, no smartwatches. calculators provided for the
quantitative items where allowed. Any unauthorized material is an
academic-integrity referral per university policy.
